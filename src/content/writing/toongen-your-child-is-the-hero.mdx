## The face that's never there

My kid has a shelf of storybooks. Animal adventures, space journeys, festival tales. She reads them, loves them, asks for them at bedtime. But the hero on every cover is always someone else. A bear, a rocket kid with generic curls, a girl lighting diyas who looks nothing like her.

She still looks. Every page, I watch her eyes search for a face that might be hers. It's never there.

We've read the same stories dozens of times. She knows every beat — when the rocket launches, when the diya lights up, when the animal finds its friend. She still pauses on the illustrations, hoping maybe this time the kid on the page will look like her. It never happens.

I kept thinking: what if the book on her shelf actually starred her? Not a cartoon filter pasted on a template. A real storybook character, drawn from her face, on every page of a printed book she could hold.

That gap stayed with me longer than I expected.

## What I built

So I built [ToonGen](https://toongen.app). The line I kept coming back to: **your child, the hero of their own story.**

You upload two or three photos. ToonGen draws your child as a storybook character — not a filter, not an avatar sticker, but someone who belongs on printed pages. Their name gets typeset into the story. You pick an adventure — Animal Adventure, Journey to the Stars, Diwali of Lights — and the book builds around them as the hero of every page.

The full book is thirty pages. A real storybook, not a slideshow or a PDF thrown together overnight.

## The parts I cared about most

I have two young kids. I would not ship something where a stranger's face shows up in my child's book, or where photos I uploaded sit on a server forever.

So the flow is deliberate. You approve the character first. Then you approve every page before anything gets printed. The photos are used only to draw the character, then deleted automatically. Nobody is guessing what your kid looks like from a permanent upload sitting in a database.

And before anyone pays, the first ten pages generate free. You can read them, show your kid, decide if the character feels right. No subscription trap, no "buy to see if it works." If the face doesn't look like your child, you find out before you spend anything.

## The object

When you're ready, the full thirty-page book ships across India — paperback or hardcover, starting from ₹1,499. A printed storybook that arrives at your door, with your child on every page. There's something different about a physical book your kid can carry to breakfast and point at themselves. Digital PDF if you prefer pixels over paper.

There's also a short animated story video — about thirty seconds, starring your child. Free to try. Sometimes my daughter wants the story to move, not just sit on a shelf.

(I also made a potty training chart with the same character on it — A3 size, ₹99 digital or ₹299 printed. It's there if you need it, but the book is the thing.)
